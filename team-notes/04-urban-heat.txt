URBAN HEAT ISLAND & MICROCLIMATE MITIGATION - THE TEAM EXPLAINER
(Everything you need to explain this idea in 2 minutes)


THE ONE-LINER
We map the city's "fever" from space, rank which neighbourhoods are worst
off, and tell the government exactly where to plant trees and paint roofs
so every rupee of public money cools the most people.

WHY IT MATTERS
Cities are hotter than the countryside - concrete and asphalt soak up the
sun, and heat waves now kill more people than floods and storms in Indian
cities. Officials want to act, but where should a limited budget go? Our
system answers that question with data instead of guesswork.

HOW IT WORKS - THE ANALOGY
A satellite carries a thermometer: it measures the actual surface
temperature of every street and roof. We draw a "fever map" of the city -
hot spots glow red. But temperature alone isn't the whole story: a hot
empty industrial park matters less than a hot crowded slum. So we combine
heat with who lives there (population, elderly), how little green cover
exists, and how built-up the area is, into one vulnerability score 0-100.
Then, like a shopper comparing prices, we compute what cools the city most
per rupee: a tree costs Rs 1,200 and cools a 25 m2 patch; a cool roof
reflects the sun; reflective pavement does the same for roads. The system
picks the best combination for each hot zone within the budget.

WHAT WE'RE BUILDING (simple pipeline)
1. Landsat passes every 16 days; thermal bands are converted into a
   real land surface temperature map (with the physics worked out).
2. We compute greenness, built-up density, water and population per zone.
3. A transparent formula (temperature 40%, green deficit 20%, people
   15%, buildings 10%, plus elderly, roads, water) scores each zone 0-100.
4. For every hot zone, an optimizer recommends interventions - cool
   roofs, trees, reflective pavement - ranked by benefit per rupee.
5. A simulator lets officials test "what if we plant 1,000 trees here?"
   and see the estimated temperature drop before spending anything.

THE DEMO STORY
A judge sees the city map glowing red in a hotspot: 43.8C, 7% green cover,
82% buildings. One click generates a mitigation plan - 18,000 m2 cool
roofs, 1,200 trees, 4,500 m2 pavement at Rs 97.65 lakh - with an estimated
2.1C cooling and the vulnerability score dropping from 87 to 69. Another
click simulates a bigger tree budget and the map updates live.

TECH, DECODED
- Land Surface Temperature (LST): real ground heat from satellite
  thermal sensors, using known physics (radiation math).
- NDVI / NDBI: greenness and built-up scores from photo colors.
- Vulnerability score: a transparent weighted points formula - every
  point can be explained to a planner.
- Optimizer: a budget-shopping algorithm (knapsack style) that picks
  interventions by benefit-per-rupee.
- Simulator: re-runs the same math with your "what if" inputs - results
  are labeled estimates, not guarantees.

KEY TALKING POINTS (say these with confidence)
1. "We map real surface temperature from space, not weather-station
   guesses."
2. "Score combines heat + people + missing greenery - hot is bad, hot
   and crowded is urgent."
3. "Optimizer tells government where each rupee cools the most people."
4. "Simulator lets officials test interventions before spending -
   'model estimate, not guaranteed outcome' keeps it honest."
